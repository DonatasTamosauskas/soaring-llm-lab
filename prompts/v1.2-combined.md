Your task is to build a VR game inspired by the likes of Gorilla Tag and Agar.io, with the core mechanic being bird-like flight. Instead of gorilla-like movement, movement is performed by flapping the wings (controllers). Changes of direction and speed are achieved by tilting the wings (controllers) to achieve different lift characteristics in a bird-like aerodynamics fashion. For faster, slower flight dynamics - changing the angle of attack of an airplane (with the temporary balooning of a change in angle of attack). For executing turns via movements of opposite tilts, akin to coordinated aileron and rudder movements. All flight dynamics inspired by real aerodynamics, however the exact forces and angles can be tuned for best gameplay instead of maximum realism. Additionally to plane-like mechanics you must develop an intuitive flapping mechanic - flapping the wings should produce lift. The direction of the lift should depend on the angle of the wings being flapped - either vertical upwards or with a slight directionality forwards. The game world should have vast open spaces and many areas for intricate flight conditions - such as tree branches, open windows, electric poles, tall buildings, nesting areas with tight air-borne entrances etc. - all the things that birds cling to and make acrobatic flight required and rewarding. The world should also include areas of updraft that bring up a bird without the need for flapping the wings. The core game loop - catch birds that are smaller than the player while avoiding being caught by birds larger than the player (the NPCs themselves are both catching other NPC birds smaller than themselves and avoiding larger birds, making the world highly dynamic). The player grows as they catch more birds, changing their flight characteristics and making their gameplay loop different (the player starts to ignore the smallest birds, care about larger pray). Build the game in godot, use all the tools at your disposal, especially godot CLI (https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/editor/command_line_tutorial.html) and make sure you only finish work once the core mechanics of flight, the game world, the game loop, and VR interactions are all perfected. Use Meta XR Simulator to test your work through a VR lens, and check out /Users/don/Projects/meta-demo for an example how to set the Simulator up. Build the game in ultracode mode, taking good advantage of sub-agents where feasible. Build a workflow that will tackle each area of the game and push it to perfection with verified/tested output in each of the areas. You must verify your work and will need to think of ways to validate your work before having access to a physical VR device (Meta Quest Pro will come in later for testing). Some of the areas are: world building, graphics (low-poly art style), assets (e.g. enemies, feel free to explore open assets such as itch.io), game loop (the progression from smallest to larger bird), enemy AI (the world should feel alive, enemies should target each other as well, not just the player character), UI (game menus, pausing, restarting, controllers, etc.), VR interactions, and anything else you feel should be included in the v1 of the game. Definition of done: perfection achieved in each area, and only consider an area complete if you were able to validate your via tests, visual output or in the game engine/meta simulator output (you will need to devise smart testing setups to achieve this for each area). At the end, when all sub areas are done the work must compile and run as a whole in VR - use the meta xr simulator for testing this.
