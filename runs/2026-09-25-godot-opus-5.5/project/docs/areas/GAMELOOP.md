# Game loop — catch smaller, avoid bigger, grow

Area: **gameloop**. Owns `scripts/game/`, the values in `scripts/core/size_rules.gd`,
`tests/unit/game/`, `scenes/dev/gameloop_*`, `tests/shots/gameloop_*`,
`artifacts/gameloop/`.

## Lesson prey (the UI's contract, core loop round)

The first-flight catch lesson asks the game for prey the player can catch
at once (the lead's direction: "a competent new player completes the catch
lesson within 1-2 minutes"). Duck-typed on the node in group `game_loop`:

| Call | Does | Returns |
|---|---|---|
| `request_lesson_prey(near: Vector3 = Vector3.INF) -> Array[Bird]` | Puts a **lesson swarm** up: five moths (`SwarmMoth`, 6 g like every swarm moth - one grows a sparrow ~25% -, real Birds caught by the normal catch rule) that never dart, in open air **45 m ahead of the player's flight at its height, but at most `MothField.LESSON_MAX_AGL` (40 m) above the ground** (`near` = the player's head by default; the direction is its velocity, or its facing when slow). While the lesson runs the target cue **prefers the lesson's moths** (`SwarmMoth.lesson`; `ThreatWatch.lesson_active`: scored 50x, looked for out to `LESSON_RANGE_M` = 120 m and all round, taken from any other target at once), so the ring and the arrow are on one as soon as it is in sight - also for a player cruising 80 m up (fix round 1; before it the ring stayed on another bird for 6-23 s, and a high player never got a ring). The Ecosystem keeps the swarm ahead of the player (placed again when the player has flown 90 m away from it or past it) until it is released. **Calling it again** while the lesson runs (the UI's help after `CATCH_HELP_S` without a catch) puts it ahead again, **12 m nearer each time** (at least 20 m out). | The moths (empty: no Ecosystem, or no open air ahead - the lesson then runs on the sky's own prey). |
| `release_lesson_prey()` | The lesson is over (a catch, its timeout, a skip): the lesson swarm leaves the sky. | - |
| `lesson_prey() -> Array[Bird]` | The lesson's moths still in play. | |

A moth is `species == &"moth"`; its model is a `BirdModel` (it has no
`glow` property: the moths carry the edible mark and, once ringed, the cue).
A new run (`start_run`, Ecosystem reset) drops the lesson swarm; ask again
if the lesson is still being taught (UIRoot does). Pinned by
`tests/unit/ai/moth_test.gd::test_the_lesson_prey_waits_ahead_of_the_player`
and, through the real game and the competent person,
`tests/unit/integration/game_catch_test.gd::test_the_catch_lesson_is_quick`.

## The rules (and why)

### Who can catch whom
- **Ratio**: predator mass >= `EAT_RATIO` (1.25) x prey mass, **at the moment of the catch**: a frame's contacts are applied in contact order and each re-checks the ratio with the masses of that moment (a player that has just eaten is not eaten later in the same frame by a bird now smaller than it).
- **Contact**: centre distance within `r_pred + r_prey + reach`. NPC reach = 0.25 x predator wingspan (0.15 on the player, below). **Player reach = 2.0 / sqrt(time_scale) wingspans** (2 spans as a sparrow, 1.46 as a pigeon, 1.0 as an eagle), x (1 + 1.25 x catch assist), and **never more than `PLAYER_CONTACT_MAX_SPANS` (2.9) wingspans centre to centre** - 4.9 felt metres in the headset (world_scale = wingspan / 1.7 m), under three arm spans.
  *Why the square root (fix round 3; round 2 used 1 / time_scale):* a small bird lives fast - in the headset a sparrow's world rushes by at ~60 felt m/s, an eagle's at ~16 - so the same felt reach is a shorter moment of contact for the sparrow. But a big bird's worthwhile prey is relatively smaller and nimbler than a small bird's (a pigeon turns in 0.4 of an eagle's radius, a wren in 0.67 of a sparrow's). In the shipped valley, with reach ~ 1 / body time, a hawk or an eagle caught 2-7% of its chases and a sparrow 29%; runs starting as an eagle caught 0.26 birds a minute, with the square root 0.50 (`artifacts/gameloop/tuning_r3/`, f1 vs e1). The felt reach now runs from 3.9 m (sparrow) to 2.1 m (eagle) unassisted; round 2's was 3.8 m to 1.3 m.
  *Why the cap:* round 2's full assist gave a sparrow 4.5 wingspans of reach, 8 felt metres - a "magnet", a feather burst far from the beak. For small birds the assist is now mostly the wider cone; for big ones its reach.
- **Aim cone**: prey inside the predator's forward cone at the moment of contact (NPC 55 deg half-angle; player 80 deg around the body heading *or* the velocity, since the arm-derived heading wobbles with every flap; **never more than 90 deg**, even with the catch assist). Bodies overlapping deeply (centres within 0.6 x summed radii) count regardless - except on the player (below).
- **The player as prey - strikes must connect** (VR forgiveness): an NPC's strike on the player needs the player inside a **55 deg** cone (no wider than NPC on NPC), a reach margin of **0.15** of the hunter's span (NPC on NPC: 0.25), and never counts by body overlap alone. A struggling player (danger assist, below) gets a narrower cone (down to 35 deg) and a shorter reach (down to half). *Why 55 (fix round 5; 40 before):* since integration round 2 the AI's attacks on the player are committed passes (the last 0.4 s holds its line, turning at most 0.8 of its rate: a well-timed break makes the hunter overshoot), and this round's threat cue names an attack the frame it starts. With both and the 40 deg cone, a strike connected about once in ten close attacks on a competent modelled player and only 23-30% of runs were ever caught (`tuning_r5/t1_1p10_cone40/`; >= 40% is the brief's "happens", pinned before round 4's runs); 50 deg gave 38% on the full tier and 29% on the Quest's (`tuning_r5/t2_shadowed_threat_watch/`), 55 deg 40-50% (`d_danger_explore/`, `t3`). The committed pass is the forgiveness now.
- **Swept, exactly**: contact is solved over the frame's relative motion ("within reach", "overlapping" and "inside a cone" are each a quadratic or linear inequality in time; every change of truth is at one of their roots: `CatchRule.contact_time`). Jumps faster than 90 m/s, and anything announced with `teleported(bird)`, are not swept.
- **Deterministic chains**: contacts are sorted by (contact time, heavier predator, heavier prey, positions) - never registration order - and applied in that order. A bird eaten earlier in the frame can neither eat nor be eaten later; a predator eats at most once per frame, then waits its handling time (NPC 1.2 s, player 0.2 s). A catch involving the player needs a run in progress when it is applied (a winning catch ends the run; a strike later in the same frame does not take the winner).
- **Cover is out of play**: a bird hiding in a refuge (`NpcBird.hidden`; `CatchRule.is_hidden`) can neither be caught nor catch until it comes out, is never a threat and never the target cue.
- **Refuges**: prey inside a `World.get_refuges()` sphere cannot be caught by a predator wider than the refuge's `max_span`. One `RefugeIndex` per world (a grid of x/z columns), shared by the catch pass and the target cue.
- **Catch assist** (for strugglers): after 30 s x sqrt(body time) without a catch the player's reach and cone grow, reaching +125% reach (within the cap) / +10 deg (capped at 90) at 120 s x sqrt(body time); any catch resets it. A sparrow gets help after 30 s, an eagle after 60 s. *Why the square root (round 2: plain body time):* a hawk then waited 2 minutes before any help and 7 for full help, and the late game's dry spells ran to 10-20 minutes - a player feels minutes, not body-seconds; the clock now scales like the reach.
- **Final-approach magnet** (core loop round, the lead's direction: "a gentle final-approach magnetism when prey is inside a forward cone at close range; a catch should feel like 'I flew into it', not a precision test"): each step, every bird the player can eat that is ahead within `MAGNET_S` (0.4 s) of flight - at most `MAGNET_SPANS` (12) of its wingspans, at least `MAGNET_MIN_M` (2.5 m) - and inside `MAGNET_CONE_DEG` (45 deg) of its velocity drifts towards the player's flight line (through its head, along its velocity), fast enough to be on it when the player arrives but never faster than `MAGNET_PULL` (0.4) of the player's speed; sideways only (it is not pulled along the line). Not a bird perched, in cover, sheltering where the player cannot follow, or protected. `GameLoop._magnet`. *Why:* through the real flight chain a person's arm-flown approach passes 0.7-1.4 m from a small bird (the wingbeat's scatter; a sparrow's reach for a moth is 0.53 m) and 86-96% of catches needed the catch assist (integration round 2); with the magnet a pass 1.2 m off a moth takes it (`mechanics_test.test_the_magnet_draws_prey_onto_the_flight_line`), and through the real chain 65-76% of the catches came without the assist (the evidence below). ChaseLab's one-on-one chases measure the catch rule and the flying without it (`ChaseLab.magnet`, off by default).

### Danger: being eaten happens but is avoidable
The AI's sky hunts the player; a person reacts in ~0.2 s, uses part of the turn envelope and cannot see behind. The loop shapes when and how hard (all tested; the numbers tuned on whole runs in the valley):
- **First flight** (core loop round): a run opens with its first flight - nothing hunts the player while it finds its wings (the UI's first-flight lessons run in it) - which ends `OPENING_AFTER_CATCH_S` (15 s) after the player's first catch (the catch lesson's), never before `OPENING_MIN_S` (60 s) and never after `OPENING_RESPITE_S` (90 s) of play (a rolling half second of respite, renewed each step). It is a respite, not protection: a player that flies into a hunter's talons can still be taken. *Why (the lead's direction: "from ~60-90 s into the first flight (after the first catch or lessons), predators periodically pick the player"):* fix round 5's first flight lasted until the first tier-up (up to 5 minutes): a slow starter flew five minutes with no danger, a fast one met its first hunter as a starling. (History: round 3 opened with 120 s after round 2's novices were eaten by a swallow in their first two minutes.)
- **Protection**: run start 3 s, respawn 5 s (+ danger assist); ends when the player catches something. Protected: uncatchable, and NPCs are told to leave it alone (meta `npc_ignore`, which the AI honours).
- **The danger director** (core loop round, the lead's direction: "predators periodically pick the player - about one telegraphed attack every 1-2 minutes at the start, scaling with tier - with an audible/visible warning early enough to evade"): outside an attack NPCs leave the player alone (meta `npc_ignore`, as before). When the respite after the last attack is over (and the player is not protected, and the first flight is over), `GameLoop._direct_danger` asks the sky for an attack - `Ecosystem.send_attacker(player)`: the best-placed bird that would hunt the player, 22 m out or more (nearest 50 m), or one brought in out of view - and calls again every `ATTACK_CALL_RETRY_S` (5 s) until one comes; the AI's hunters may also go for the player of their own accord while the window is open. The attacker is named, marked and shown from the moment it sets off (the warning, below). **One sent attacker at a time** (second pass): while the bird last sent still hunts the player and no attack has begun, nobody else is called, for up to `ATTACK_SENT_WAIT_S` (30 s; the 5 s retry had sent a second and a third hunter while the first approached). Once the attack is over (every bird hunting the player quiet for 1 s, or the player shook the attacker off: the escape grace) the next respite runs: **`ATTACK_RESPITE_S` (110 s) at the first species, falling linearly in tiers to `ATTACK_RESPITE_APEX` (0.55) of it at the apex** (`attack_respite(mass)`: 110 s as a sparrow, 88.8 s as a pigeon, 60.5 s as an eagle; second pass: at 85 s, with the first attack now on time, the competent person was caught twice a run and the pigeon slid to 8:25 - `cl3_it10`), x the danger assist's factor, x boldness: **every further attack survived since the last death shortens the next respite by `BOLD_DECAY` (0.85), down to `BOLD_FLOOR` (0.6) of it**; being caught resets that. `get_run_stats()` reports `attacks` (an attack reached attack strength), `attacks_sent` and `respite_left`. *Why:* fix round 5's respite (12 s x body time) spaced the attacks the AI's planned threats happened to make - through the real flight chain the person was caught in 6 of 8 full-tier runs but 2 of 8 at the Quest tier, whose 28-NPC sky has few raptors near the player; the director brings attacks at either budget (the evidence: first hunter 60-90 s in, ~0.7-0.9 attacks a minute over the first ten minutes, caught at least once in most runs at both tiers, no run lost).
- (Fix round 5's *a sparser sky's hunters come back sooner* - every respite x (budget / 60) - is gone: the director brings the attacks at any budget.)
- **Respawn respite**: after being caught the player is left alone for **`RESPAWN_RESPITE_S` (60 s)** (x the danger assist: 2 minutes after a first death) - a life lost buys time to regroup. Novices lost whole runs to a second hunter a minute after the first.
- **First-tier grace** (fix round 5, integration round 2's proposal adopted; core loop round: from the first tier-up itself): the moment the player first grows out of its first species - whenever it does - NPCs leave it alone **35 s** (`FIRST_TIER_GRACE_S`; 45 s in fix round 5): the new size is the player's to enjoy before anything hunts it (the brief: not punished within 30 s). *Why:* through the real flight chain the respite had ended half a second after the "Now a Swallow!" card, and in 4 of 10 runs a crow took the new size away 9-13 s later. *Why 35, not 45:* with the first flight now ending 60-90 s in, a first tier-up at ~1:45 held the sky's first attack back past 2:30 (`cl_it6`).
- **Escape grace**: when a predator's strike at the player misses - its reach came within 1 of its wingspans and it is past, 2.5 wingspans away, with no catch - the player is protected for 2.5 s (+ danger assist), a respite starts, and `escaped(predator)` fires. **Only a bird hunting the player (or within 3 s of hunting it) makes an attack or a strike to escape** (second pass: `ThreatWatch.hunt_peak_level`; a crow merely named for flying close past a sparrow in its first flight counted as an attack shaken off and started the full respite - the sky's first real attack came at 2:30-2:45 instead of 60-90 s).
- **Danger assist** (the catch assist's twin): each death adds 0.5 (max 1), decaying with a 180 body-second time constant of play. At full assist: respawn protection 15 s, escape grace 8.5 s, strike cone on the player 20 deg (from 40), strike reach half, every respite x3 (`DANGER_ASSIST_RESPITE` 2; x5 before the director), the threat cue's horizon x1.6 (it warns earlier), and the death penalty halved (15% of mass, not 30%: a player caught again and again is not also pushed back down the ladder again and again). A player who is rarely caught never sees it.
- **Eat to heal**: besides a new peak tier, **every 5th worthwhile catch since a life was last lost gives it back** (up to 3). A struggler stuck at one size wins lives back by playing the loop. *Why:* the valley's modelled novices sat 10-15 minutes as starlings, eaten by pigeons and crows every 3-5 minutes, and lost 4 runs in 10 at a slower growth tuning (`tuning_r3/g2_1p4`).
- **Warnings that are true and early** (the threat cue below): it reads intent, warns earlier for bigger birds, and - core loop round - shows an attacker from the moment it sets off (`warn_level`).
- **What hunts you** (`get_run_stats().danger_species`, for the UI): every ladder species that can eat the player **plus the species of every live bird that can** - at the top of the ladder the AI sends eagles heavier than the player after it, and the screen must not say "nothing hunts you" then (round 2's list was from the ladder only). *Correction (fix round 5 review):* round 4's doc said the HUD and the pause screen read this list; they do not - `PauseScreen.food_chain` and `HUD.tier_up_text` use the ladder only, so an eagle's pause screen says "Nothing hunts you" while heavier eagles hunt it. A request to UI is in the contract note.

### Growth
Two separate questions, two formulas (`scripts/core/size_rules.gd`):
- **Is it worth chasing?** `meal_value = 0.8 x (prey/eater) x smoothstep(0.03, 0.25, prey/eater)` - size-independent. **Worthwhile when >= 2%** (prey heavier than roughly a tenth of you). As you grow, a species' ratio falls and it drops off the menu (moth at 40 g, wren at 120 g, sparrow at 300 g, swallow at 550 g, starling at 1 kg, pigeon at 3 kg). Highlights, the target cue, the apex count and the AI's prey choice use `is_worthwhile`.
- **How much does it grow you?** `meal_gain = meal_value x your mass x growth_efficiency(mass)`, with `growth_efficiency = GROWTH_GAIN x (mass / 30 g)^-GROWTH_SIZE_EXP` - **1.8 / 0.42** (core loop fix round 1, tuned through the real chain on tuning seeds 101-106 once the buffet was gone - `fx1_it1`-`fx1_it4`; the core loop round's 1.15 / 0.32 then put the pigeon at 8-10 minutes). A swarm moth (6 g) grows a sparrow ~25%; an eagle's best meal is worth 0.23 of a sparrow's (`growth_test` pins < 0.95). `meal_worth` = actual growth fraction (HUD/summary). *Why the exponent:* pellets and the magnet made small prey easy through the real chain (2 catches a minute), so the long haul from pigeon to eagle has to come from the meals: at a flat efficiency (1.5 / 0.0, `cl_it4`) the eagle came at 9-12 minutes. The exact values sit between two tuning batches (`cl3_it10`: 1.1 / 0.31, pigeon ~8:25; `cl3_it11`: 1.3 / 0.36, pigeon ~5:15) - "The evidence".
- **A sparser sky grows you more per meal**: each of the player's meals is multiplied by `sky_growth(mass)` = (60 / the Ecosystem's `max_npcs`)^(`SKY_GROWTH_EXP` x ramp), at most x2 (x1 at the full budget or with no Ecosystem; `get_run_stats().sky_growth`). **Fix round 1: 0.08 and flat (`SKY_GROWTH_FLOOR` 1): x1.06 at the Quest's 28 NPCs, x1.09 at the governor's 20** (the core loop round's 0.55 - x1.52). With the pellets, the director and the swallow time the Quest's sky is no longer short of meals: at x1.52 (`fx1_it3`) and x1.36 (`fx1_hold1`) the Quest tier climbed 1.2-1.5x faster than the full tier at the same base growth. Through the real chain the Quest sky runs short of the small birds a swallow or a starling grows on (1.6 catches a minute from the swallow to the pigeon against the full tier's 3.5; pellets are dust by then), and fix round 5's ramp (0.3 as a sparrow rising to 1 as an eagle) helped least exactly there: the Quest's pigeon came at 8:21 against the full tier's 4:10 (`cl3_it9`). The better fix is still a denser sky near the player at small budgets (the AI's population plan); then the factor goes back towards 1 with the same evidence.
- The player's species follows mass; `Events.player_grew` once per meal; `player_tier_changed(old, new)` exactly once per change; a new peak tier gives a life back (max 3), as does every 5th worthwhile catch since a life was lost. Growth caps at 1.5 x eagle mass (4.5 kg).
- **NPC eaters keep 50% of the gain, capped at +4% of their spawn mass** (was +15%): the AI spawns a species within +-9% of its ladder mass, so the heaviest fed bird weighs at most 1.09 x 1.04 / 0.91 = 1.246 x the lightest - under `EAT_RATIO`. With +15% a well-fed NPC eagle could eat a player who had just become the eagle, and NPC sparrows each other (review).

### Species ladder
moth 4 g, wren 12 g, sparrow 30 g, swallow 55 g, starling 100 g, pigeon 300 g, crow 500 g, gull 850 g, hawk 1.3 kg, eagle 3.0 kg; `wingspan_for_mass` is continuous.

### Death, lives, run end, restart
- Caught -> `Events.bird_caught`, `Events.player_caught(pred)`, player `alive = false`, controls off, cues cleared, `Game.CAUGHT` for a **2.5 s beat** -> penalty (-30% mass, cut by the danger assist - 22.5% after a first death -, never below 30 g; may drop a tier) -> respawn at `World.get_player_spawn()` via `PlayerBird.respawn()` with protection and the respawn respite -> `Game.PLAYING`.
- **3 lives**, won back by a new peak tier or 5 worthwhile catches (eat to heal); the last catch ends the run after its beat: `Game.ENDED`, `Events.run_ended(summary)`.
- **Pause**: the loop is PROCESS_MODE_PAUSABLE and its timers run on its own clock, so the beat, protection and respites freeze; resuming mid-beat returns to CAUGHT.
- **Restart** (`restart_run()`, from any state) resets the player, lives, stats, apex, catch assist, danger assist, boldness, the eat-to-heal count, respites (a new first flight opens), per-bird tracks, cues/highlights, `Game.run_time`, and `Ecosystem.reset()`.
- UI-driven transitions: MENU/ENDED -> PLAYING starts a run (also straight after a continued, endless run); PLAYING -> MENU abandons it (records still saved).

### Apex goal
Reaching the eagle fires `apex_reached()`. The goal: **3 worthwhile catches while an eagle** (`apex_progress(n, 3)`) -> `victory(summary)`, `Game.ENDED`, summary `reason = victory`; `continue_after_victory()` resumes endless play (the run clock is kept). The top of the ladder is not safe: the AI keeps a threat above the player (eagles heavier than it), so the apex is "become the eagle, and rule the sky while bigger eagles hunt you" - and `danger_species` says so. `reign_time` counts play as the eagle (HUD, summary).

### Threat and target (UI / audio / haptics)
- **Threat** of a predator = time-to-contact term `(1 - ttc / horizon)` weighted by whether it is pointed at the player (unaimed x0.55), or a proximity floor (up to 0.4 within 6 predator spans), **x intent** (a bird hunting someone else, or nothing, counts at 0.35). The **horizon is 3.5 s x body time** (x1.6 at full danger assist). A bird in cover is no threat. **The warning** (core loop round): a bird hunting the player within `warn_range` (60 m, or 40 of its wingspans) is shown at least at `warn_level` (0.2) from the moment it sets off - named, marked, the HUD's arrow (from 0.12) and a quiet heartbeat (audio, from 0.02) - under attack strength (0.3: announced, not yet an attack); the urgent cues come with the attack itself as its time to contact falls (the haptic pulse at 0.35, the predator's scream at 0.6). *Why the floor under attack strength:* tried at 0.35 over 60 m, the whole approach counted as an attack - the modelled person evaded ~20% of its play from hunters 40-60 m out, no attack got within a wingspan and nothing was ever eaten (`cl_it2`).
- **One named predator, at its own level** (fix round 4). Each predator's raw threat is smoothed **on its own** (asymmetric low-pass, rise 0.06 s / fall 0.4 s, slew limits 6/s up, 0.9/s down). The cue (`Events.threat_changed(level, predator)`, which the HUD arrow, the haptics and the predator's call follow) names one bird and reports **that bird's own smoothed level** - never another's.
  - **A real attack is named at once** (fix round 5): a bird **hunting the player** (intent in full) at **attack strength** (0.3, `announce_level`) with a live raw threat takes the name the frame it gets there - unless the named bird is itself a live attack that strikes at least as soon. It takes it when the named bird is quiet, **harmless right now** (its own raw threat under 0.1: it has made its pass and turned away, its level only decaying), or **not hunting the player** (a bystander, whatever it shows); and from another live attacker when it **will strike sooner** - time-to-contact shorter by `strike_margin` (0.04 of the horizon: 0.14 s for a sparrow) and its level above the named bird's threat right now - or when its level is above that by 0.15. Among several, the one striking soonest.
  - **Against birds that are not attacking, the name is sticky**: a rival takes it when its level beats the named one's by **0.15 for 0.5 s** (`switch_margin`, `switch_hold_s`), or when the named one has been **quiet** (under 0.1, `QUIET_LEVEL` - the UI's "real threat" line) for 0.5 s; any rival at attack strength takes a quiet name at once; the name **never moves by preference to a bird that is no threat right now** (own raw threat under 0.1); a named bird that **leaves the game** is replaced at once (by the soonest striker if there is one), at the next bird's own level.
  - The attack respite reads `hunt_peak_level` (the worst smoothed level of any bird hunting the player, or within 3 s of hunting it: an attack starts when one reaches 0.3 and ends when all have been under 0.05 for 1 s; `peak_level`, every predator's, is for the charts).
  *Why (fix round 4 review, major):* round 3 low-passed the *worst* raw threat and held the name only while the named bird's raw threat stayed above 0. An attacker that jinked for half a second handed its name, and its attack-strength level, to whichever bystander was nearby: in the valley the arrow, the haptics and the call hopped hunter -> bystander -> hunter every ~0.6 s during attacks. A jink still never hands the name to a bystander: a bystander is not hunting the player.
  *Why the attack rule (fix round 5 review, major):* round 4's hold also held **real** attacks back behind a faintly shown bird - a non-hunting bystander at 0.1-0.35, or a hunter already flying off - while a bird hunting the player climbed to 0.4-0.8 unnamed (the review's live probe: 516 masked episodes in 58 attack-minutes; 441 of 525 attacks that began behind another name named more than 0.25 s late, 29 never), and with another hunter named at 0.66 a second one stooping from behind was named 0.13 s before contact. Now the review's probes pass as written (the hunter named the frame it reaches attack strength behind a shown bystander; the second hunter named 1.05-2.38 s before contact), and in the valley no live attack is ever masked (results). `threat_naming.png` re-enacts round 4's and round 3's rules next to this one on one scenario.
- **Target** = the best worthwhile prey within max(45 spans, **6 s of flight** - core loop round; 4 s before) and 110 deg of heading, by worth x **how catchable it is** (`chase_odds`) x **how readily it comes to a chase** (`prey_ease`, core loop round: a pellet - a swarm moth - 1, a bird 0.3; through the real chain the person caught 30-55% of its chases of swarm moths and 8-29% of small birds') x height (prey below can be dived on) / distance x facing; **never a bird in cover, sheltering where the player is too wide to follow, or out of the player's reach in open air** (core loop round, the lead's direction: "only reachable prey is ever the target cue: require open-air line of sight and an approach path - no committing to birds inside crowns/hedges; drop a target that becomes hidden instead of holding it"): a ray from the player's head to it against the world's static geometry must be clear AND a sphere of `reach_clear` (1.2 m, or 3 of the player's wingspans) round the bird must touch nothing (a crown, a hedge, a wall it perches on); both are cast only for a bird that would matter and cached 0.25 s, and **a target that fails either is dropped at once** - at the next look, no grace (`SIGHT_GRACE_S` 0; fix round 5 held it 1 s, a 2 s version wedged a person following the ring at the geometry again and again). `last_target_change` says `sight` or `clutter`. **Never switched away while the player is gaining on it** (fix round 5): from the moment a target is named, every time the player gets 5% closer to it than it has been, it is committed for `target_commit_s` (2.5 s x the square root of body time: 5 s for an eagle). Otherwise kept until invalid (eaten, hidden, sheltered, out of range or reach, no longer worth it: replaced at once), or beaten by **2x after a 4 s hold**. *Why the open-air rule:* through the real flight chain the ringed bird sat 15-37 m away inside tree crowns, the catch lesson timed out (300 s) and integration's whole-game catch test went from 3 catches to 0 with 63 of 77 chases wedged (the previous agent's 05:27 edit, which held a target through 2 s out of sight; reverted before this round).
  *Why (fix round 5 review, major; integration round 2):* the cue moved on from a still-valid bird ~9 times a minute (median 2.2 s after naming it, 65% within 3 s, the abandoned bird 32 wingspans away), and through the real flight chain 55-80% of the chases of a person following it ended because it named another bird. The modelled pilot now follows the cue, also when it moves on mid-chase (it used to keep its own target, so the evidence never paid for the switches); `mechanics_test.test_the_target_cue_follows_the_chase_in_whole_runs` and `pacing_test.test_the_target_cue_stays_on_a_chase_in_the_sky` measure it in whole runs.
  *Why the sight test (fix round 3):* in the valley two in three of a big player's chases ended with the prey out of sight within seconds - the cue was pointing behind houses, crowns and cliffs. Over whole runs the modelled competent player's chases succeeded 15% of the time with it and 8.5% without (`tuning_r3/g_a` vs `g_b`, same seeds).
  *Why `chase_odds` falls to 0.05 from 0.65 of the player's mass:* in the valley near-equals were caught in 2-4% of chases against 10-55% for prey at a tenth to a third of the player; with round 2's floor (0.1 at 0.8) a near-equal's bigger worth made it the cue's pick - a pigeon-sized player was pointed at other pigeons 350 times for 6 catches.
- **Highlights**: 1 = worthwhile prey, 2 = **a bird hunting the player** (core loop round, the lead's direction: "danger marks only on birds actually hunting the player, or named as the threat, or within 3 s of hunting it - never on the harmless murmuration or every bigger bird; the attacker must stand out"): a bird is marked 2 while it hunts the player and for `DANGER_MARK_HOLD_S` (3 s) after (a hunter breaking off and coming round again stays marked; a bird that shows no intent counts as hunting); the cue names a hunter the frame it sets off (the warning), so the named attacker is marked; a bird named for merely passing close is not; the murmuration never hunts the player (the AI), so it is never marked. Within max(70 spans, 6 s of flight) (15% hysteresis); birds that die are unlit on the next frame. The UI also marks the cue's target and threat (by its own rule: 2 on the named predator only from level 0.1, 0 on a bird it stops naming); **the loop re-writes its own value on those birds right after emitting the cue events**, so every frame ends with the loop's classification. *Why:* marks on every bird that could eat the player covered the starling murmuration round a sparrow (8-12 marks in `sky_*_murmuration_zoom.png`, 64-69% of the marks in view) and the real attacker did not stand out (integration round 2; its proposal marked birds hunting anyone - this marks only the player's hunters).

## Known limits
- **The evidence is a modelled person through the real chain, not a person.** Integration's person (SimPilot's decisions: reaction times, evasion, give-up rules) flies the real PlayerBird with scripted arm motion; it does not learn, and it follows the ring. The first headset sessions decide the feel; the numbers here decide only that the loop can meet the brief.
- **Pinned against the rock: fixed for the cliff, not for rooms.** The wall-air boundary condition (`PlayerBird._wall_wind`) frees a bird the valley's wind held against the west cliff (`pin_escape_test`). A person who flies *into* a village room (a novice's swallow in `vcl1_nov` 413, 9 minutes) still has to find the window again: the harness person's unstick does not (it steers to the most open ray, which in a room is the room); a real player can see the way out. The wind blows indoors too (1.4 m/s in that room) - the world's wind field is not bounded by walls.
- **Medians of six.** The brief's windows hold on the held-out medians; single runs spread wide (full pigeon 2:34-7:24, Quest 4:55-8:56; eagle 20:12-32:18 / never), and the Quest's eagle median (29:31, 4 of 6) sits at the window's edge. A new held-out block could land a median a minute either way.
- **The Quest tier's pacing rests on a growth factor, not a denser sky** (`sky_growth()`, now only x1.06 at 28 NPCs, x1.09 at the governor's 20, not measured at 20). The better fix is the AI's population plan keeping the small birds a swallow grows on at the 60-NPC density near the player.
- **Feasts are paced, not prevented**: a player can still take several birds of the murmuration in a row, one per swallow (0.2-1.2 s), but for 30 s after a tier-up meals grow it only to just under the next species, so a feast never skips a species or stacks celebrations (fix round 1).
- **`sky_hunt_test` fails at the Quest tier on its seed 13** (1 readable hunt in 300 s, 39 staged-hunt tries found no pair; bar 3). On seeds 21 and 29 it passes at both tiers (Quest 9 and 5, full 9 and 9): one seed of a sky whose spread is large (the test's own note: 1-7 a flight); this round's changes moved that sky. Not re-seeded.
- **Not addressed from the fix round 1 reviews (minors, UI/birds)**: coral threat chevrons for a non-hunting bird near the player during the lessons (level 0.1-0.2 by proximity); no notice explains the chevron at the first attack; the catch lesson's "Nice!" card is see-through and its title changes as the lesson prey is released; rings and chevrons use two colour languages; swarm moths read as a sparse scatter of rings.
- **The mirror** (`scripts/game/sim/`) is a snapshot of the AI from before its fix round 1; it is the replayable regression and the danger mechanics' test bed, not evidence for the brief.
- **Cross-area**: the apex celebration, the CAUGHT beat's presentation and `escaped()` feedback are UI/audio's (the loop emits the facts). **Highlights have two writers (loop and UI)** - the loop re-writes its own value on the birds each cue event names, right after emitting it. `danger_species` is not read by the UI's pause screen or tier card yet (request in the contract note).
- **A restart is a reset, not a tier change**: `restart_run()` puts the player back to a sparrow without `player_tier_changed` (listeners resync on `run_started`).
- **Catches register 3-5 felt metres from the head**, and the final-approach magnet draws small prey onto the flight line: generous on purpose (VR forgiveness); to be judged in the first headset sessions (`CatchRule.player_reach`, `PLAYER_CONTACT_MAX_SPANS`, `GameLoop.MAGNET_*`).
- **Perf** (GDScript, dev Mac): catch pass ~140 µs and watch ~150-200 µs per frame for 60 birds in the unit tests; in the held-out real-chain runs the median frame's catch pass was ~180 µs and watch ~136 µs at the full tier, ~105 / ~83 µs at the Quest tier. Expect 2-3x on the Quest's CPU.
